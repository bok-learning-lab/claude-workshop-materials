# Course Websites with Interactive Demos

A deployed multi-page Next.js app anchored to a specific course's canonical material — its stills, its sonnets, its source images — paired with two or three interactive demos that let students explore the course's concepts parametrically rather than just read about them. Most course websites are syllabus + readings + a Canvas link. The pattern catalogued here is different. The site is a real piece of software with a landing page, dynamic markdown-driven content routes, and concept demos built around the canonical objects of the course. The student manipulates a parameter — a lighting angle, a trait rubric, a strength slider — and watches a concept that would otherwise be abstract become legible on material the course has already taught.

The architectural common ground is small and stable: Next.js, Vercel, a flat-file `_content/` directory that becomes a sidebar-navigated docs site through a single dynamic route, and one or two demo pages whose data file is the source of truth. The pedagogical common ground is sharper. Anchor the demo to material the student has already encountered in section. Show the student what the model sees — no obscured prompts, no rewriting. Make the framework's limits *structurally* legible rather than hidden in a footnote. The three projects below instantiate the pattern in three different registers: a cinema workshop with no LLM call anywhere, a literature unit whose three demos each fail differently and where the contrast is the lesson, and a generative-image studio whose critical rules sit in the sidebar with UI controls literally tied to specific rules.

## Projects in this category

- **[Film course concepts website — *Rashomon* / GENED 1049](film-rashomon/)** — Prof. Alex Csiszar's East Asian Cinema workshop. A top-down diagram of Kazuo Miyagawa's three-point lighting choices on three *Rashomon* stills, with key/fill/back angles and intensities encoded as data the student can read at a glance. A second demo plays a course scene that the page scrolls past, with editing-overlay notes that fade in and out tied to scroll position. No LLM call on the site itself.
- **[Literature course concept website — Love Songs / CompLit 126x](literature-lovesongs/)** — Prof. Moira Weigel's Unit II on voice. Three demos, in order: trait-rubric scoring of three sonnets, then sonnet generation from the scores alone with no poem text; then full-source prompt stuffing with three Shakespeare sonnets; then voice-cloning the student's own draft read aloud in Weigel's voice. Each demo fails differently. The contrast is the lesson.
- **[Image API widget — The Virtual Camera](image-api-widget/)** — a provider-agnostic Stable Diffusion widget paired with Alexander Kluge's "A Few Preliminary Rules" rendered in the UI sidebar. Specific controls are tied to specific rules. The optional source-image input is labeled with Rule 1 (presence of source information); the strength slider's middle band is named *"dialog"* after Rule 2.
- **Story-map template — *Art of Monsoon Asia* / HAA 91** — the pattern's static, platform-neutral variant, from the June 8–11 cohort. Students compile primary-source sites and objects in a shared Google Sheet across the semester; the template turns those rows into an interactive Leaflet story map (curated artifacts placed in space and time) that can stand alone as a GitHub Pages site or be lifted into Scalar or ArcGIS StoryMaps later — the faculty member hadn't yet chosen a platform, so the deliverable refuses to choose for them. Source: [`claude-code-20260609/recipes/faculty-recipes/monsoon-storymap/`](https://github.com/bok-learning-lab/claude-code-20260609/tree/master/recipes/faculty-recipes/monsoon-storymap).

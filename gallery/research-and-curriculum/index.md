# Research & Curriculum Synthesis

This category collects projects where the **inputs are institutional, policy, or research-in-progress documents** and the **outputs are concrete recommendations or navigable sites** for sharing the work with colleagues, deans, department chairs, or the public. They are not source-to-teaching pipelines — those live in other categories of this gallery. The audience for the outputs here is *peers and stakeholders*, not students in your classroom.

The two projects below run in opposite directions across the same intellectual terrain. One is **synthesis**: pulling together a normative framework, an institutional case for change, and an empirical research base, and applying them as discipline-specific curricular recommendations a department chair or instructor could act on. The other is **publishing**: taking a researcher's working files — analyses, drafts, primary sources, AI-generated drafts — and putting them behind a friendly viewer so colleagues can navigate the research-in-progress without you re-authoring any of it as web content.

What they share is the underlying commitment: the documents you already work with — the Bok Center guidance you already cite, the markdown notes you already keep — should be operable directly. No CMS in the middle, no rewriting into "best-practice boilerplate," no copying-and-pasting into a Substack template. The Claude-assisted move is to make those documents act on a specific target (a department, a syllabus) or to make them visible to a specific audience (the public reading along with your research) without the usual ceremony in between.

## Projects in this category

- **[Research paper site](research-paper-site/)** — about 150 lines of Next.js code that publish a researcher's working files as a navigable public site. Drawn from the *Harvest Times* site for an investigation into Anglo-Saxon harvest-time pharmacology.
- **[Recentering Academics](recentering-academics/)** — turns three layers of source material (the Bok Center's *Recentering Academics* initiative, Harvard's institutional case for change, external empirical research on grading) into discipline-specific curricular recommendations. Demonstrated against Linguistics.

## When to use this pattern

Reach for these patterns when you have **written material that should be doing work** — guidance documents you want operationalized at the syllabus level, research notes you want readable by colleagues, policies you want translated into actionable redesign — and the bottleneck is the labor of mediation, not the substance of the material itself. The category's bet is that the mediation labor (drafting recommendations against published guidance; turning a research folder into a website) is where Claude Code earns its keep, *if* you can keep the recommendations grounded in the specific target and keep the site honest to what the researcher actually wrote.

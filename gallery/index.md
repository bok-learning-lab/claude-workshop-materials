# Project Gallery

<div class="page-lead">Twenty worked examples from across two cohorts of the Bok Center workshop, grouped into six pattern families. Each is a real faculty build — with prompts, inputs, and outputs you can read in place and lift for your own course.</div>

The gallery is organized by **what the project does**, not by who built it or which day it ran. The point is that the same authoring move — a faculty member, Claude Code, an afternoon — is now available across disciplines that would otherwise be hard to compare. The categories below name the moves; the specific projects underneath are instances of them.

Specific course names, faculty names, and source corpora are kept where they sharpen the example (the *Rashomon* stills make the lighting-diagram demo what it is; Becca Belofsky's discrete-math interactives are not a generic PhET demo). Where a project is built around a particular professor's paper or course, that is named. Where a pattern is generic enough to lift wholesale, the page says so explicitly.

## [1. Interactive Concept Simulations]({{ site.baseurl }}/gallery/interactive-simulations/)

PhET-style single-file HTML widgets — small, manipulable, conceptually disciplined worlds in which a learning idea becomes visible. The kind of artifact that historically required a development team is now within reach of a faculty member with an afternoon.

## [2. Course Websites with Interactive Demos]({{ site.baseurl }}/gallery/course-websites/)

Deployed multi-page Next.js apps anchored to specific course material: a landing page, two or three interactive demos tied to the canonical texts or images of the course, reference content for students. Students explore the course's concepts parametrically rather than just reading about them.

<span class="tag">Next.js</span> <span class="tag">Vercel</span> <span class="tag">course material</span> <span class="tag">demos</span>

## [3. Practice & Assessment Tools]({{ site.baseurl }}/gallery/practice-and-assessment/)

Tools faculty use to generate assessments and tools students use to rehearse for them. Designed to *coach* rather than *grade* — the human stays the moral agent.

<span class="tag">assessment</span> <span class="tag">skills</span> <span class="tag">webapps</span>

## [4. Source → Teaching Materials]({{ site.baseurl }}/gallery/source-to-teaching/)

Distillation pipelines that turn primary sources — a paper, a lecture transcript, a folder of papers, a set of lecture notes — into pedagogical artifacts: case studies, top-ten takeaways, illustrated lectures, summary docs with explicit research-agenda twists.

<span class="tag">distillation</span> <span class="tag">handouts</span> <span class="tag">case studies</span>

---

## [5. Reading at Scale]({{ site.baseurl }}/gallery/reading-at-scale/)

Using an LLM as a *close reader at corpus scale* — refusing grep, refusing keyword matches, instead reading each text fully and surfacing structured findings. Works for song lyrics, dense modernist novels, comparative translation, qualitative interview corpora, and primary-source philology.

<span class="tag">corpus analysis</span> <span class="tag">structured output</span> <span class="tag">humanities</span> <span class="tag">qualitative methods</span>

---

## [6. Research & Curriculum Synthesis]({{ site.baseurl }}/gallery/research-and-curriculum/)

Projects whose inputs are institutional, policy, or research-in-progress documents — and whose outputs are concrete recommendations or navigable sites for sharing the work. The Bok Center's *Recentering Academics* initiative made workable as department-level syllabus revisions; a researcher's working folder made a public-facing site with ~150 lines of code.

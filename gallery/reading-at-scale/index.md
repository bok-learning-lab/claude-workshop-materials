# Reading at Scale

A corpus too large for one human read — 538 Bob Dylan songs, the 28,500 lines of *Finnegans Wake*, fourteen translations of the *Odyssey*, a stack of qualitative interview transcripts, Calvino's five completed *Memos* — and a move that is genuinely a *reading*, not a count. Keyword search misses too much: possessives but not enjambed pairs, direct spellings but not puns and avatars, surface vocabulary but not underlying reasoning. The instruction at the heart of each project below is the same: **close reading, at corpus scale, by an LLM that has been told to read like a close reader and refuse to grep.** Every finding is paired with the exact line it appears in. The quote is the receipt. A scholar can audit the output without re-reading the corpus.

Half the work is naming what *not* to count. Bob Dylan's lyrics contain dozens of words ("frost," "pound," "swift") that coincide with writers' surnames; the exclusion list is what separates a usable finding from an impressive-looking pile of junk. *Finnegans Wake* hides Fionn mac Cumhaill in puns and landscape metaphors any keyword filter would dismiss; dropping search entirely and shrinking the chunks until one agent can read every word is what surfaces the references. Mary Waters' flexible coding refuses the "LLM discovers themes" framing — the LLM applies the *researcher's* concepts at scale, surfacing candidates for human review. The output is structured: JSON with named fields per finding (line reference, matched text, reasoning, confidence, variant type), plus a prose writeup that does the interpretive work the JSON indexes. The prose is where the reading happens; the JSON is the substrate.

## Projects in this category

- **[Smart text search — Bob Dylan](smart-text-search-dylan/)** — one prompt, 538 songs, asked to name every writer mentioned in any lyric. The pass turned up 14 named writers across 10 songs: Woody Guthrie, Tom Paine, Ezra Pound, T.S. Eliot, Verlaine and Rimbaud, Nietzsche, Wilhelm Reich, F. Scott Fitzgerald — each with the verbatim line they appear in. The *"Verlaine's and Rimbaud's"* rule (when a line names two or more, list every one) is the discipline that makes the output trustworthy.
- **[Smart text search — *Finnegans Wake*](smart-text-search-joyce/)** — built to support Prof. Natasha Sumner's *Heroes of the Gael: A History of Fionn and the Fianna* (Harvard, Feb. 2026). 48 close-reader sub-agents, each judging a ~600-line slice in full, surfaced 277 references to Fionn mac Cumhaill across the puns, misspellings, avatars, and landscape allusions that defeat keyword search.
- **[Text analysis — Calvino's *Memos*](text-analysis-calvino/)** — Italo Calvino's *Six Memos for the Next Millennium* by the numbers: eight hand-rolled SVG bar charts, a 2D semantic-embedding map of every paragraph of all five lectures, a draft composer that runs the same analyses on a student's prose live. The student writes the unwritten sixth memo — Consistency, the one Calvino died before drafting — and sees their prose plotted in Calvino's space, side by side.
- **[Interview coding (Mary Waters' flexible coding)]({{ site.baseurl }}/gallery/day-4/project-overviews/interview-coding/summary/)** — three skills implementing Deterding & Waters' three-stage workflow: `/index-transcript` for indexing, `/find-negative-cases` for the validation move, `/methods-paragraph` for transparent reporting. The skills refuse to "discover themes"; they apply the researcher's codes at scale and surface candidates for human review.
- **Video corpus builder** — the corpus that isn't text yet, from the June 8–11 cohort. Videos plus subtitles become a searchable, video-linked language corpus: search a word, see every utterance containing it (with speaker and timestamp), click to play the video at that exact moment. Modeled on the CORAAL Explorer's search → concordance → media-player loop, and grown directly from a faculty "Project as Recipe" card. Source: [`claude-code-20260609/recipes/faculty-recipes/video-corpus-builder/`](https://github.com/bok-learning-lab/claude-code-20260609/tree/master/recipes/faculty-recipes/video-corpus-builder).
- **[Texts and translation]({{ site.baseurl }}/gallery/day-4/project-overviews/texts-and-translation-v0/summary/)** — the *Odyssey* in fourteen translations (Greek, Latin, Spanish, French, Swedish, eight English) plus Jagannātha Paṇḍitarāja's seventeenth-century Sanskrit *Rasagaṅgādhara*. Skills for comparative-translation views and candidate-identification of *alaṃkāras*. The treatise that defines the figures also audits the skill's calls.

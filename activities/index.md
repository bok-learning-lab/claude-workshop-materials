# Moves & Activities

<div class="page-lead">Short exercises for building intuitions about how AI works — what it's good at, where it stumbles, and why. Most take 15–30 minutes and work in Claude.ai or Claude Code.</div>

---

## [01 · Close Reading]({{ site.baseurl }}/activities/01-close-reading/)

Use Claude to do close readings of Shakespeare scenes — then compare Claude's analysis with your own. What does it catch? What does it flatten? A good early exercise in treating Claude as a reading *partner*, not an authority.

<span class="tag">Claude.ai</span> <span class="tag">literary analysis</span>

---

## [02 · Population Pyramids]({{ site.baseurl }}/activities/02-population-pyramids/)

Feed Claude UN World Population Prospect data and ask it to produce visualizations. Then try asking it to do the same arithmetic by hand — and notice when it fails. This is the clearest demonstration of when to reach for code over prose.

<span class="tag">Claude Code</span> <span class="tag">data visualization</span> <span class="tag">tool use</span>

---

## [03 · Recipes]({{ site.baseurl }}/activities/03-recipes/)

A gentle introduction to multimodal prompting: give Claude a photo, a list of ingredients, or a partial recipe and see how it builds from context. Also surfaces useful failure modes around specificity.

<span class="tag">Claude.ai</span> <span class="tag">multimodal</span> <span class="tag">prompting</span>

---

> **On failure modes:** Activities 01 and 02 are deliberately designed around moments where Claude *doesn't* do well — asking it to do multiplication, seeing it hallucinate authorship, etc. These aren't bugs to avoid; they're the most useful things in the curriculum.
